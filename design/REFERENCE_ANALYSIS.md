# Reference analysis (2026-11-14)

Four sites were opened and scrolled in a browser on 2026-11-14. Notes are
from what was actually on screen that day. These are sources of principles,
not templates. No graphic, logo, layout or asset is copied.

## DeepBook (deepbook-v2-wip.webflow.io)

What is there: a near-black page, one saturated accent used as a solid block
(not a gradient), a very large two-line headline centered low in the hero,
two buttons of different weight directly under it, and a structured product
section that scrolls through "Spot / Margin / Predict" with a 3D object on a
white card. Section labels are small. Logos of partner teams appear in a
dedicated "Who's building" strip.

Principles to adopt:
- One accent, used as a flat surface, never as a glow.
- A hero that is mostly type and negative space, with the headline carrying
  the page. The visual sits beside or below it, not behind it.
- Product content presented as a numbered, scrolling structure.

Adapted for PineLaunch: the accent is pine green and the surfaces are green
tinted charcoal, so flat pine panels replace the blue blocks. The structure
becomes the three weeks (Idea, Build, Launch).

## Onlines (onlines.com.ar)

What is there: a white canvas with a faint grid, a single huge word
("internet.") and dozens of small real objects scattered around it: tool
icons, cursors with role labels (Desarrollador, Cliente, Director creativo),
a browser window, a phone, a photo. They drift in as the page loads and
respond to the pointer. The typography is bold grotesque, extremely tight.

Principles to adopt:
- The hero shows real things from the subject's world, not abstractions.
- Playfulness comes from recognizable objects placed with care, each one
  readable on its own.
- Motion is one orchestrated entrance, not continuous spinning.

Adapted for PineLaunch: the objects become the student's own project in its
three states (a sketch, a working build, a launched page) plus small,
readable interface pieces. No floating shapes that mean nothing.

## Giga (giga.ai)

What is there: true black with thin vertical column rules running the full
page height, a bright orange accent used only for buttons and small markers,
monospace uppercase section labels, large light-weight headlines, and
interface panels that look like the real product (a chat window with a
cursor, an email input, a "the customer asks" transcript). Industry tabs
are plain text rows. Motion is mostly state changes inside the product
panels.

Principles to adopt:
- Column rules as the only background structure, instead of a grid wash.
- Monospace labels for metadata, in moderation.
- Interface panels that are the product, built with real controls and
  readable text.
- Accent reserved for the few things you can press.

Adapted for PineLaunch: faint vertical rules on the home page only, pine
green on primary buttons and the current step, and project mockups that
look like something a 15 year old could actually ship.

## Pier Gorelli (piergorelli.com)

What is there: a "plotting" loading state, then a dark page with blueprint
annotations: section numbers in brackets ("[01] MANIFESTO"), figure labels
("FIG. 01 · WIREFRAME → PRODUCTION"), construction overlays with measurements
and hex values on case study images, a stepped type scale, and a warm
off-white mode. Case studies alternate text column and figure.

Principles to adopt:
- Technical annotation as a visual language: figure numbers, small measured
  labels, hairline rules.
- Figures that explain how a thing was built.
- Alternating editorial rows (text / figure) instead of a card grid.

Adapted for PineLaunch: the three-week process is shown as one project
figure annotated across three states. The showcase uses figure labels
("Fig. 1 Mobile app") so it is clearly illustrative. Annotation stays
light so a parent does not feel lost.

## Combined direction

"One project, three states." Every student makes one thing. The home page
hero shows that one thing moving from a sketch (Idea), to a working screen
(Build), to a live page with a link (Launch). The same three-state idea
drives the timeline, the dashboard and the week pages, so the site has one
story instead of decoration.

Rules that follow from this:
1. Visual quality comes from interface mockups, type and composition.
   No orbs, node graphs or grid washes. Faint column rules at most.
2. One accent (pine). It marks what is pressable or current.
3. Space Grotesk carries the headlines at large sizes with tight tracking.
   Manrope does the reading. IBM Plex Mono is reserved for figure labels,
   dates and step codes.
4. Motion: one entrance sequence in the hero, state changes inside panels,
   progress fills, and a page fade. Nothing rotates forever.
5. Layouts alternate: editorial two-column, a full-width figure, a timeline,
   a quiet reading column. Not the same card repeated.
6. Everything shown as student work is labeled as an example.
